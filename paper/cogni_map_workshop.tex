\begin{abstract}
Theory of Mind (ToM)—the ability to reason about others' mental states—is crucial for AI alignment and human-AI interaction, yet its mechanistic implementation in language models remains opaque. We introduce Cogni Map, a mechanistic interpretability framework that decomposes ToM into constituent cognitive processes using linear probes trained on 45 cognitive actions. By combining probe-based detection with Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) steering vectors trained on perceptual contrasts from the BigToM benchmark, we achieve both ToM performance improvements (45\% to 56\% accuracy) and mechanistic insights: steering systematically increases perceptual attention (\textit{emotion\_perception}, \textit{noticing}) and hypothesis formation while reducing explicit interrogation. This demonstrates how cognitive action decomposition can bridge representation engineering and cognitive science, offering interpretable analysis of high-level AI capabilities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Theory of Mind (ToM)—the capacity to attribute mental states to oneself and others—represents a critical capability for AI systems engaged in social reasoning, alignment, and human collaboration \cite{bereska2024mechanistic}. While recent work has demonstrated that large language models (LLMs) can perform ToM tasks \cite{chen2024designing}, the \textit{mechanistic basis} of this ability remains unclear: What cognitive processes constitute ToM reasoning? How do internal representations shift when perspective-taking is engaged?

Traditional evaluations treat ToM as a monolithic capability measured through binary accuracy on belief attribution tasks. However, ToM in cognitive science comprises multiple interacting processes: perceptual encoding, hypothesis generation, emotional inference, and counterfactual reasoning. Understanding which components are active—and how steering vectors modulate them—would enable principled improvement of AI social cognition and earlier detection of reasoning failures.

We introduce Cogni Map, a mechanistic interpretability framework combining two techniques: (1) linear probes trained on a 45-action cognitive taxonomy to detect active reasoning processes, and (2) Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) steering vectors to enhance ToM while measuring resulting cognitive shifts. This approach moves beyond "does the model have capability X?" to "how is capability X mechanistically implemented?"

\textbf{Contributions:} (1) A cognitive action taxonomy with 45 categories and 31,500 training examples; (2) Binary probes achieving 0.78 AUC-ROC with layer specialization peaking at layer 9; (3) CAA steering vectors trained on perceptual contrasts from BigToM, improving accuracy 11 percentage points; (4) Mechanistic decomposition revealing ToM operates through perceptual attention and hypothesis formation rather than explicit interrogation.

\section{Methodology}

\textbf{Cognitive Action Taxonomy.} A taxonomy of 45 cognitive actions was defined across four categories: \textit{Metacognitive} (e.g., \textit{reconsidering}), \textit{Analytical} (e.g., \textit{analyzing}), \textit{Creative} (e.g., \textit{reframing}), and \textit{Emotional} (e.g., \textit{emotional\_reappraisal}).

\textbf{Activation Capture and Probing.} Following established probing methodologies \cite{alain2016understanding,chen2024designing}, activations were extracted from 30 of the 34 layers of Gemma-3-4B using nnsight. During both activation capture and inference, inputs were augmented with the suffix "The cognitive action being demonstrated here is" to ensure consistent extraction from the final token representation. A \textbf{one-vs-rest} strategy was used to train 45 independent binary linear probes, which allows for per-action interpretability and the flexibility to mix optimal layers for each action during inference. Training was performed with an AdamW optimizer, cosine annealing scheduler, and early stopping, using an AUC-ROC metric to handle the severe class imbalance.

\textbf{CAA Steering Vector Training.} To isolate ToM-specific cognitive processes, we trained Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) steering vectors \cite{rimsky2023steering} using the representation engineering framework \cite{zou2023representation}. We constructed 752 contrastive triplets from BigToM's \textit{forward\_belief} scenarios (both true and false belief conditions). Each triplet contains: (1) story context with observational information (protagonist sees or does not see an event), (2) belief attribution question, (3) positive completion: correct belief attribution, (4) negative completion: incorrect belief attribution. This captures the representational difference between accurate and inaccurate ToM reasoning rather than surface-level observational statements. Vectors were trained across layers 14-30 using PCA-centered activation differences, exported in GGUF format, and applied with coefficient=1.5.

\textbf{ToM Decomposition Methodology.} We evaluated ToM reasoning on 100 samples from the BigToM procedural benchmark, comparing baseline (no steering) vs. steered conditions. For each sample containing a story, question, true answer, and wrong answer, we captured cognitive action activations at three timepoints: (1) after story+question (before answer), (2) after story+question+true answer, and (3) after story+question+wrong answer. Answer selection used probability ranking over multiple-choice letters (a/b), following BigToM methodology. For each cognitive action, we computed layer count (number of layers 10-20 where probe confidence exceeded threshold 0.001) and analyzed differences between baseline and steered conditions to identify which cognitive processes are modulated by ToM enhancement.

\section{Results}

\textbf{Probe Performance.} Binary probes achieved 0.78 average AUC-ROC and 0.68 F1 across all 45 actions, confirming linear separability of cognitive representations \cite{alain2016understanding}. Performance ranged from \textit{suspending\_judgment} (0.988 AUC) and \textit{counterfactual\_reasoning} (0.984 AUC) to \textit{emotion\_responding} (0.778 AUC). Layer 9 showed peak performance (0.948 AUC), with strong mid-layer performance (layers 5-24) and degraded early/late layers, suggesting early layers encode low-level features while mid-layers capture semantic abstractions. This informed our layer 10-20 analysis window.

\textbf{ToM Steering Efficacy.} CAA steering (coefficient=1.5) on 100 BigToM samples improved accuracy from 45\% to 56\%. Correct answer probability increased from 0.446 to 0.560, while incorrect decreased from 0.554 to 0.440, confirming perceptual contrast training enhances ToM without explicit prompting.

\textbf{Cognitive Decomposition of ToM.} ToM steering systematically modulated cognitive action patterns. At question timepoint: \textit{emotion\_perception} (+2.35 layer count), \textit{noticing} (+1.59), \textit{hypothesis\_generation} (+1.57), \textit{emotion\_valuing} (+0.94), \textit{suspending\_judgment} (+0.82), \textit{questioning} (-1.01). After true answer: \textit{emotion\_perception} (+1.48), \textit{noticing} (+1.24), \textit{hypothesis\_generation} (+1.18), \textit{suspending\_judgment} (+1.09), \textit{convergent\_thinking} (-1.48), \textit{questioning} (-1.27), \textit{understanding} (-1.02). Patterns indicate heightened perceptual attention and hypothesis formation with reduced explicit interrogation.

\textbf{[PLACEHOLDER: Full-Scale Results.]} The complete evaluation across all 12 BigToM conditions (forward/backward belief/action × true/false belief) and systematic analysis of layer-wise activation patterns, timepoint dependencies, and cognitive action correlations are currently in progress. Preliminary results suggest condition-specific cognitive profiles, with false belief tasks requiring enhanced \textit{counterfactual\_reasoning} and backward inference tasks engaging \textit{metacognitive\_monitoring}.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Mechanistic Insights.} Our decomposition reveals ToM operates through perceptual grounding rather than deliberate reasoning. Increases in \textit{emotion\_perception} and \textit{noticing} coupled with decreases in \textit{questioning} and \textit{convergent\_thinking} suggest a shift from effortful "cold" cognition to automatic "hot" social cognition—challenging assumptions that LLM ToM relies primarily on chain-of-thought reasoning. This mirrors cognitive science distinctions between explicit and implicit perspective-taking.

\textbf{Methodological Contribution.} Using perceptual contrasts (``sees event'' vs. ``does not see event'') to train CAA vectors isolates ToM-specific processing without instruction contamination, enabling precise attribution of cognitive shifts to perspective-taking mechanisms rather than general task improvement \cite{rimsky2023steering}. This approach generalizes to other capabilities requiring mechanistic decomposition.

\textbf{Limitations.} Synthetic probe training data, single model evaluation (Gemma-3-4B), preliminary single-condition results (100 \textit{forward\_belief\_true\_belief} samples), independence assumptions between cognitive actions, and layer count metrics that may not capture activation magnitude.

\textbf{Future Work.} Full 12-condition BigToM evaluation, layer-wise magnitude analysis, temporal dynamics across measurement timepoints, extension to commonsense reasoning/moral judgment/deception detection, human annotation validation, and scaling to larger models.

\textbf{Broader Impact.} Process-based supervision via cognitive monitoring enables early detection of reasoning failures for AI safety. Understanding constituent processes of capabilities like deception or persuasion allows targeted steering interventions for alignment. Applications in education and mental health require responsible deployment guidelines to prevent manipulation via emotional optimization.

\section{Conclusion}

Cogni Map demonstrates that complex AI capabilities can be mechanistically decomposed into interpretable cognitive components. Our framework combines linear probing for process detection with CAA steering for targeted intervention, revealing that ToM enhancement operates through perceptual grounding and implicit inference rather than deliberate reasoning. This bridges mechanistic interpretability and cognitive science, offering principled methodology for understanding and steering AI reasoning processes. The toolkit, probes, and datasets enable extension to other high-level capabilities including commonsense reasoning, moral judgment, and deception detection.

\subsection{CAA Training Data Example}

The CAA steering vectors were trained on contrastive pairs distinguishing correct from incorrect belief attribution. Each training triplet consists of a story with observational information, a question about the protagonist's belief, and two completions representing accurate vs. inaccurate Theory of Mind reasoning.

\textbf{False Belief Example:}

\textit{Story:} ``Noor is working as a barista at a busy coffee shop. Noor wants to make a delicious cappuccino for a customer who asked for oat milk. Noor grabs a milk pitcher and fills it with oat milk. A coworker, who didn't hear the customer's request, swaps the oat milk in the pitcher with almond milk while Noor is attending to another task. Noor does not see her coworker swapping the milk.''

\textit{Question:} ``Does Noor believe the milk pitcher contains oat milk or almond milk?''

\textit{Positive (correct ToM):} ``Noor believes the milk pitcher contains oat milk.''

\textit{Negative (incorrect ToM):} ``Noor believes the milk pitcher contains almond milk.''

\textbf{True Belief Example:}

\textit{Story:} ``Noor is working as a barista at a busy coffee shop. Noor wants to make a delicious cappuccino for a customer who asked for oat milk. Noor grabs a milk pitcher and fills it with oat milk. A coworker, who didn't hear the customer's request, swaps the oat milk in the pitcher with almond milk while Noor is attending to another task. Noor sees her coworker swapping the milk.''

\textit{Question:} ``Does Noor believe the milk pitcher contains oat milk or almond milk?''

\textit{Positive (correct ToM):} ``Noor believes the milk pitcher contains almond milk.''

\textit{Negative (incorrect ToM):} ``Noor believes the milk pitcher contains oat milk.''

The key distinction is that the positive completion always reflects accurate perspective-taking (understanding what the protagonist actually believes based on their observational access), while the negative completion represents a ToM failure (confusing the protagonist's belief with objective reality or making incorrect inferences).
